our story

It all started with a dream
A dream to contribute to the healing of our beloved Earth. 
A dream to create a self-sufficient little paradise for humans to live in harmony with nature. 
A dream to treat it as a temple, a place to remember we are but children in the arms of something Greater. 

Finally home
And so it went. In 2024 we found it. We fell in love with it at first sight. This was it, our new Home. An abandoned and bewildered farm in a moonlike landscape. This was the highest village on the mountain and it seemed a world on its own.  
We felt like we had won the lottery. Burned-out from living a way too busy life in Northern Europe we could finally breathe again. It took us a year to conclude our lives in Belgium and make the final move. The renovating of the granite stone ruin was about half way. We had started to cut the bush back, opening up the space and discovering trees and more terraces. 

Our apocalypse
And then, 3 weeks before we were supposed to move in, the largest wildfire ever recorded in Portugal came our way. For 3 days the sun didn’t pierce through the ash-loaden clouds, showing but a red glow. It was snowing ashes and the air was hard to breathe. The fire line was huge and moved quickly. We heard that the firemen said this fire was mad. It seemed unstoppable. I was afraid that the kids would get traumatized if they would see it, so we decided to leave the area and went to friends. We heard the fire had arrived to the village and no firemen were there to receive it. The people barricaded inside their homes or hurried to throw buckets of water at their olive trees. The neighbor stood at his gate seeing how the fire got around our house and decided to drive his tractor through the burning bushes to extinguish the flames around our house to save it. It was still in a fragile state as it hadn’t been finished yet. This was the miracle that saved our house. A courageous man. 

More miracles
The land had been transformed into a war zone. The green had turned into black. But when we went back 2 days later I witnessed the miracles and with every little miracle my joy grew into this strange euphoria of gratitude. The house was untouched. 75% of my veg-garden was still alive. The shed where we had stored the piano had been surrounded by flames but didn’t burn. Most of our big trees didn’t burn to ashes. Our Mother ChesnutTree - the tree where I felt the soul of this land for the first time when I fell in love with it - even protected our dining table standing underneath. It seemed that all where we had put love in had been protected! But all our building materials had burned, all our preparations vanished in ashes. They hadn’t received the love…yet. 
I felt very silent inside, my spirit was strong. The devastation was overwhelming but I was held in a big embrace of the many who had prayed and cared for us. 

10 days later the roots of the mimosa trees were still boiling hot. I had noticed smoke during the morning and knew it would become flames when the wind came up in the afternoon. I called the firemen but they refused to come for smoke. By the time the smoke had become flames I called them again but nobody came. We had to take care of it ourselves. I had tried to save the kids from the image of the flames and now they were carrying buckets of water up the mountain to try to master it. And then, by grace, I had the impulse to drive to the village once more to see if the firemen had arrived. I saw the yellow car of the forest patrol driving by. I ran behind it and they came to help us out. 

From ashes to mud
This was the start of a challenging period, the aftermath of the fire, that had started with more fire, followed by another 6 dry weeks of drought and eating ashes. When the rains finally came, they came abundant. Very abundant. With the vegetation burned away and the ashes forming a hydrophilic layer, the rains just glided down the slopes without being absorbed and slowed down. We found ourselves in the midst of a big black mudriver. No channel we dug was able to contain it. 

A spiritual process of transformation
For many years, digging into the depths of my soul, I had encountered the image of total destruction at the root of my fears. In this image I was hovering above a settlement, observing how all I had ever belonged to had been devastated. And here I was, looking at the blackened reset, an almost familiar feeling arose as if devastation was part of me. But in this moment something had changed. I was not alone, I was not devastated. I was being carried in a bigger love and courage called Faith.

I remember myself looking at how the land was being destructed and the soil was getting washed away, and knowing that one single thought could take me down a spiral of negativity. I refrained. I didn’t let myself think it. I could only observe and let my thoughts go to the mountain, to the Mother. She had gone through all this many times before. I let her deal with it. So I surrendered. 

It was the beginning of a long process of baptism into this new reality. We had dreamed to live closer to the elements and here it was, the elements in full force. The water was being followed by a hurricane in January. The shed that had protected the piano from the flames now blew away and we had to invent a rescue operation to get the piano inside. But that was it, we escaped the worst. I listened to the wind for many hours, trying to find peace with it. Thinking that if we could become friends it might be nice to us. To be honest, it still is not my friend. One morning while waking up I got the image I need to meet the power that drives it, not the symptom of the wind itself, but the greater force of life within it and let that nurture me with potential. 
​
Finding Trust when there is a lot to fear
And so it goes, little by little we are being transformed by the elements. 
They make us feel incredibly humble as human beings in the face of nature. Small in the hands of mighty forces. I had to give up trying to control everything (two tennis elbows taught me the lesson). Surrendering, but not giving up. Finding deep trust in Life, in regeneration, in resilience. I had to learn to fall asleep in the arms of Mother Nature to not drown in fear. 
We no longer looked at what we’ve lost, but at the riches we have every day - in the silence, in the discovery, in the wonder. We find joy in creating together, even as transience watches on. I’ve learned to embrace whatever life brings—again and again, deeper into the heart.
We enjoy life with the sundial. We enjoy the delicious harvest from local farmers at the market. We enjoy the warm welcome from our new Portuguese friends. We feel at home here.

Celebrations of comfort
Little by little we built more comfort. Joeri built a tiny kitchen-bathroom area which we relied upon for the first year. Every step forward was celebrated. The first pump running (yeay no more buckets!), the first solar panels (we can load our batteries and eat mixed soup!), the first room with a woodstove (just amazing!), the big solar setup is ready (after a year without we now have a fridge again!).
We discovered how little we need to be happy and how much comfort can be appreciated. 
With the seasons also the plants came back. The spring brought so many wild flowers, the seed bank hadn't been lost! Nor did the land loose its magic. People who came to volunteer or to visit, even in the first weeks after the fire, all had that moment where they stopped in their tracks, becoming silent, to look at the land and the view, to say: what an amazing place this is. It is! There is something special about this mountain.

​It is a place of resilience and regeneration. A place where to regain inner coordinates to navigate towards what truly matters. A place to remember the stories that are not told. A place to experience raw beauty, real liveliness. 

Our off-grid spirit
Living off-grid—not just in terms of location, but also in spirit. Freeing ourself from fear-driven thinking. Disconnecting from the “earthly disease” of thinking in terms of limitations rather than possibilities, of playing it safe rather than daring to trust.
This lifestyle helps us discover even more deeply who we are, as the confrontations with ourselves, with nature, and with one another are encounters of compelling honesty. It is a lifestyle that anchors a spirit of life into reality.

The courage to keep on dreaming
While we further unfold our off grid setup with water purification systems, plant fire retardant barriers, more fruit trees and create our gardens, we learn about life and about ourselves. And we wish to inspire you to have the courage to dream and to live your dream-life. And to cultivate most of all...Trust!
